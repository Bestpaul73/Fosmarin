Mission & Vision

Why FOSMARIN exists — and where it's heading.

Mission

FOSMARIN strengthens the preparedness, response and recovery capacity of Europe's subsea cable operators and authorities by turning the fibre already in the ground into a continuous monitoring and threat-assessment layer — using Distributed Acoustic Sensing and predictive analytics to detect anchor drags, seismic activity and vessel-related threats in real time, and to give operators the forecast and decision-support tools this call asks for.

Vision

A Europe where large-scale disruption to subsea data and energy infrastructure is rare, quickly detected, and quickly recovered from — because every cable route carries its own built-in early-warning system, feeding interdependency maps, forecasting tools, and post-incident investigation data back to the operators, national authorities, and EU coordination bodies responsible for critical infrastructure resilience.

Why this matters

Europe's digital and energy lifelines run along the seabed — almost unwatched.

More than 95% of intercontinental data, and a growing share of Europe's offshore power, travels through subsea cables. They cross thousands of kilometres of ocean floor that no one can watch continuously — until now.

In simple words

Imagine a very, very long guitar string lying on the bottom of the sea.

If you pluck it anywhere — even hundreds of kilometres away — the sound travels all the way down the string. FOSMARIN listens to that string every single metre, all the time.

The "string" is a cable that's already down there, quietly carrying internet data. FOSMARIN shines a tiny flash of laser light into it and listens to the echo. Anything that shakes the cable — a ship, a dragging anchor, even the ground shifting — changes that echo. A computer instantly works out what made the sound, where, and how worried we should be.

How it works

From a flash of light to a real-time alert — in four steps.

This is Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS): the same glass fibre that carries your data becomes a continuous line of listening points, every few metres, along its entire length.

01

A laser sends tiny pulses down the fibre

Every few metres, a flash of light travels along the cable and reflects tiny amounts of itself back home, like a very fast echo.

02

Vibrations change the echo

A ship's engine, a dragging anchor or a seismic shift all shake the fibre a tiny amount — enough to change the pattern of light bouncing back.

03

Thousands of virtual microphones appear

Software turns every metre of fibre into its own listening point — so one strand of glass becomes thousands of underwater "ears" at once.

04

Predictive analytics tells threats apart

An AI model compares each sound's fingerprint to what's normal for that stretch of seabed, and flags real threats in under a second — cutting false alarms.

What FOSMARIN hears

Every threat has its own acoustic signature.

The system is trained to recognise the distinct "sound" of different events along the cable route, so operators know not just that something happened, but what.

Physical threat

Dragging anchors

A sudden, sustained low-frequency scrape moving along the seabed — the most common cause of cable damage worldwide.

Natural hazard

Seismic activity

Sharp, broadband ground movement consistent with an earthquake or submarine landslide, flagged instantly for early warning.

Vessel activity

Loitering or slow-passing ships

A steady rhythmic hum with an unusual speed or dwell time directly above the cable corridor — often a precursor signal.

Deliberate interference

Tampering & unauthorised diving

A brief, irregular spike right at the cable followed by silence — distinct from natural currents or marine life.

Project objectives · Horizon Europe HORIZON-CL3-2025-01-INFRA-01

Six objectives, aligned to the call.

FOSMARIN is an Innovation Action responding directly to the expected outcomes of HORIZON-CL3-2025-01-INFRA-01 — improved preparedness for, response to, and recovery from large-scale disruptions of critical infrastructures.

01

Resilience to natural & human-induced disruption

Detect anchor drags, seismic activity, vessel activity and tampering along the full cable route in real time, hardening subsea infrastructure against natural hazards and intentional or accidental harm alike.

02

Cross-border interdependency mapping

Map how cable routes, landing stations and connected power and data infrastructure interlink across member states and sea basins, so operators and authorities can manage cascading, cross-sectoral risk.

03

Predictive risk & forecasting tools

Give operators and authorities improved monitoring, risk and threat assessment, and forecasting tools — classifying each acoustic signature with high confidence to flag real threats before disruption occurs.

04

Post-incident investigation capability

Preserve a continuous, cable-wide acoustic record so investigators can reconstruct exactly what happened, where and when after an incident — strengthening crisis prevention, preparedness and response.

05

Stress-tested & validated at sea

Deliver digital tools for virtual and physical stress tests, then validate the system on live cable routes across multiple European sea basins using real operator and incident data.

06

Training for operators & first responders

Develop training curricula and operational playbooks so critical infrastructure operators, national authorities and first responders can interpret alerts and act on them with confidence.

Expected impact

What FOSMARIN leaves behind.

Beyond the alerts themselves, FOSMARIN is designed to change how Europe designs, regulates and trusts its subsea infrastructure — across industry, science and society.

High impact
Operators · Regulators · Governments

A blueprint operators and regulators can act on

FOSMARIN's know-how becomes a lasting resource for cable operators, regulators, authorities and governments across Europe — shaping technology choices, operations and legislation for years after the project ends. It builds on existing guidance for subsea threats, adding what FOAS makes newly possible, and sets out requirements for the next generation of cables so future infrastructure can take full advantage of the findings.

Delivered throughProject whitepapers, stakeholder workshops and policy recommendations, aimed at lasting improvements to cable design and the policy framework.
High impact
Scientific community

Ten open datasets to build the next generation of prediction tools

FOSMARIN aims to lay the ground for new prediction technologies and methods through peer-reviewed publications and ten clean, high-quality FOAS datasets released to the research community. Half will capture FOAS recordings of generated threat scenarios, cross-referenced against several reference sensors and full metadata. The other half will be curated, preprocessed FOAS data from a long-term installation, matched against reference AIS data — giving researchers real-world data to test new tracking, classification and threat-prediction algorithms.

Delivered throughTen published FOAS datasets, split between generated threat scenarios and long-term installation data paired with reference AIS vessel tracking.
Medium–high impact
Society & public trust

Fewer disruptions, and a reason to trust the seabed again

If FOSMARIN's approach is deployed at scale, its clearest societal benefit is fewer disruptions to the critical infrastructure people rely on every day. A second, harder-to-measure impact is rebuilding public confidence: recent media coverage of anchor-drag incidents and cable damage has visibly shaken how much people trust Europe's ability to protect its own subsea infrastructure. FOSMARIN addresses that directly, by being open about its progress.

Delivered throughSocial media presence, this project website, and press releases published throughout the project.
